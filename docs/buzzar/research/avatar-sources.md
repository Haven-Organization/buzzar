## 2. Where VRChat users get free avatars, and whether Buzzar can use them

| Source | Typical license | Convert to VRM/GLB for another app? | Redistribute in OSS repo? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Booth.pm free avatars/bases** | Per-creator terms, very often generated from the **[VN3 License](https://www.vn3.org/)** template. VN3 is a volunteer-made, lawyer-reviewed terms *generator*. Each creator picks per item whether online-service/avatar use is allowed (often per named platform), whether modification is allowed, and whether redistribution is allowed. Others use custom "VRChat use only" terms. | **Depends on each item's terms.** Many VN3 terms allow "use as an avatar on online services" generally, and then converting it yourself for personal use is fine. Others name VRChat only, and then it is not allowed. | **Almost always no.** Unmodified and modified redistribution are typically set to prohibited, and VN3 defaults forbid redistribution. | Downloads need a pixiv login, so they are not direct links. A user can bring their *own purchased copy* into Buzzar only if the item's terms allow use on other platforms. Buzzar must not ship it. |
| **VRChat public avatar worlds / pedestals** | VRChat ToS ([hello.vrchat.com/legal](https://hello.vrchat.com/legal)) | **No.** Uploaded avatars are not downloadable as source. "Ripping" means extracting asset bundles from the client cache, which breaches the ToS. §13.2(c): may not "share, publicly Post, copy, or distribute another User's User Content without their permission". §13.2(i): may not "harvest, or mine User Content". §13.2(k): may not reverse engineer/decompile any Client. §9.2(h)(iv): purchased marketplace content "may not export Seller Content or use it in any other context". §8.3 grants only access and use "as permitted by these Terms and the functionality of the Platform". | **No** | Ripping is also generally copyright infringement against the base author. Buzzar should never offer import from VRChat asset bundles or caches. |
| **Gumroad / Jinxxy / Payhip free bases** | Per-creator EULA/ToS, no platform-wide license. The patterns seen vary: some say "VRChat and other compatible platforms", others say "any use outside of this platform is strictly prohibited". Redistribution of originals or edits is near-universally banned. | Case by case. Read each product's ToS. | **No** (unless a specific product says CC0/CC-BY) | Treat as user-supplied content only. |
| **VRoid Hub** | Per-model conditions stored in the VRM meta and shown on the model page: avatar use (only me / authorized users / everyone), violent and sexual expression, corporate and personal commercial use, **redistribution**, **alterations**, and credit. Content protection: Hub ToS bans reverse-engineering model data. Many models are download-disabled and usable only through apps integrated with the VRoid Hub SDK. (Mostly secondary: the official ToS and developer-guideline pages block automated fetch.) | A downloadable model whose "avatar use: everyone" is set may be used in Buzzar by the user. "Alterations: allow" is needed to convert VRM0 to VRM1 or GLB beyond technical changes. | Only when the meta says redistribution is allowed **and** the license is compatible (CC0 / CC-BY / VPL with redistribution) | Buzzar's loader should **read and surface VRM meta** (VRM0 `allowedUserName`, `licenseName`; VRM1 `avatarPermission`, `allowRedistribution`, `modification`, `creditNotation`) and show credit. |
| **Unity Asset Store** | [Asset Store Terms/EULA](https://unity.com/legal/as-terms). §2.2.1: license "solely" to incorporate the Asset into a Licensed Product "such that the Asset does not comprise a substantial portion"; distribution only "as incorporated and embedded in that Licensed Product". §9.2: all other rights reserved. | Converting for use inside your own product is generally allowed. | **No.** Raw assets in a public repo are standalone distribution, and an avatar picker makes the asset the substantial part. | Some Asset Store items carry their own open license; check each one. |

## 3. Conversion workflow (VRChat Unity project to VRM)

**UniVRM (in Unity)**
- Docs: [install](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm/install/), [export](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm/export/univrm_export/), [how to make a VRM](https://vrm.dev/en/vrm/how_to_make_vrm/), [humanoid](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm/humanoid/humanoid_overview/), [shaders](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm/shaders/), [UniVRM 1.0](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm1/). Since v0.81 UniVRM is split into UniGLTF/VRMShaders and VRM (1.0) packages, with separate "Export VRM-0.x" and "Export VRM-1.0" menus.
- Typical steps:
  1. Duplicate the avatar prefab into a clean project, or remove VRChat SDK, Modular Avatar and VRCFury components after baking them. Keep the original prefab for reference.
  2. Rig must be Unity Humanoid in T-pose; use the exporter's pose/normalize option.
  3. Fill the meta: author, title, and **license permissions consistent with the base model's license**.
  4. Remap materials to MToon, Unlit or Standard.
  5. Map visemes and blinks into BlendShape clips (0.x) or Expressions (1.0).
  6. Recreate secondary motion as Spring Bones.
- Community tools exist that do this automatically: SoulFlame VRM Exporter (handles lilToon/Poiyomi and PhysBone conversion) and "VRM Supporter" for VSeeFace. Mogura VR coverage ([1](https://www.moguravr.com/?p=379404)) and Booth listings are secondary sources, so verify them before recommending.
- A known pitfall from UniVRM issues: export NullReferenceException on VRChat avatars, and spring bones rotated 90 degrees. One guide advises the plain "Export to VRM 0.x" path ([christine.website guide](https://christine.website/blog/vrchat-avatar-to-vrm-vtubing-2022-01-02)).

**VRM Add-on for Blender** ([vrm-addon-for-blender.info](https://vrm-addon-for-blender.info/en-us/), by saturday06)
- Imports and exports VRM 0.x and 1.0 and supports Blender 2.93 through 5.3. It handles humanoid bone assignment, MToon materials and spring bones. Tutorial: [create humanoid VRM from scratch](https://vrm-addon-for-blender.info/en-us/create-humanoid-vrm-from-scratch/).
- The 100Avatars VRMs were exported with it (`saturday06_blender_vrm_exporter_experimental_2.4.8`).
- This route suits creators whose source is FBX or .blend (for example, a Booth base whose license allows other platforms).

**Known limitations to tell users about**
- **Shaders:** Poiyomi and lilToon have no VRM equivalent. They degrade to MToon, so you lose rim effects, AudioLink, dissolve, matcaps, outlines tuned differently, and so on. Buzzar renders MToon (via three-vrm-style materials) or unlit/PBR.
- **PhysBones to spring bones:** this is an approximate mapping. PhysBone limits, stretch, grab/pose and collider shapes such as capsules or planes don't map 1:1. VRM1 springBone has sphere and capsule colliders; VRM0 has spheres only.
- **Toggles, Animator FX layers, gestures, contacts and parameters:** none of these exist in VRM. Only a fixed default state is exported. Expressions are limited to presets plus custom morph/material binds. Bake the desired outfit first.
- **Constraints:** VRChat or Unity constraints map to VRMC_node_constraint (1.0 only; roll/aim/rotation) where possible.
- **Licensing:** the base model's terms govern. Most VRChat bases forbid use on other platforms or redistribution, so a converted file is for that user's personal use at most. Buzzar should never host a public avatar gallery of converted Booth avatars.

## Caveats
- The 100Avatars CC0 rests on Polygonal Mind's own CC0 repo (README plus the CC0 License.md), cross-checked against the CC0 `licenseName` embedded in each file. The original 2019 repo's "don't sell unmodified" request predates the CC0 grant.
- VRM Public License assets are **not** Apache-2.0 and may not be relicensed. Downstream forks must keep the meta and credit.
- Mixamo stock content and RPM avatars should not go in the repo.
- Booth, Gumroad, Jinxxy and VRoid Hub content is per-creator. At most, treat it as user-uploaded content gated on the embedded VRM meta permissions.
- Unverified or secondary: the VRoid Hub ToS wording (official pages return 403 to automated fetch), the Mixamo raw-file ban (community FAQ, not an Adobe legal page), Quaternius/Kenney rig naming, and the PhysBone auto-converter tools.
